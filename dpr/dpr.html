2. Introduction to Bio coal Pellets, Project Goal, Background and Rationale

Particular Detail
Bio Coal Pellets Bio coal pellet is a renewable, green, eco-friendly, solid, cylindrical shaped fuel produced by compressing biomass like agricultural and forestry waste into high-density, energy-concentrated pellets under high pressure. The natural lignin present in biomass acts as a binder eliminating the need for chemical additives. It is a replacement for thermal coal and can be used by industries that burn thermal coal for heat.
Project Goal This project aims to establish a modern, scalable, reliable Bio Coal manufacturing plant utilizing locally available biomass such as Paddy Husk, Paddy Straw, Wheat Straw and Mustard Stalk to produce Non-Torrefied Bio Coal Pellets- a sustainable alternative to thermal coal for near by Power Plants (NTPC Nabinagar, SJVN Chausa) and other industries (Dalmia Cement Plant Banjari Rohtas), etc.
Why Agri Residue Based While bio-coal pellets can be manufactured from wood, utilizing agricultural residue is a far more sustainable and locally abundant alternative. Our region generates vast quantities of crop waste—primarily paddy husk, paddy straw, wheat straw, and mustard stalk—a significant portion of which is traditionally eliminated through open field burning. In line with the Government of India’s aggressive renewable energy initiatives promoting agricultural waste utilization, converting this residue into bio-coal pellets turns a massive environmental challenge into a strategic economic opportunity. This model creates a circular economy that mitigates air pollution, provides farmers with a reliable secondary income stream, and directly supports national energy security by reducing reliance on imported coal and conserving vital foreign exchange reserves.
Why Non Torrefied We are choosing Non-Torrefied Pellets over torrefied ones. Torrefaction involves 'roasting' biomass in the absence of oxygen to remove moisture and volatile compounds, turning it into a coal-like material. In contrast, non-torrefied pellets are simply compressed biomass; they retain volatile compounds that burn rapidly and they can easily absorb moisture. Mechanically, the technology for non-torrefied pellets is operationally simpler, proven, less capital-intensive, mature, and economically viable in India. Conversely, torrefaction technology is still in its pilot stages, commercially unproven locally, and rife with complex operational hurdles.
Other Applications of Bio Coal Pellets Apart from co-firing with coal in Power Generation Bio Coal Pellets can be used in Industrial boilers, steam generation plants, textile dyeing units, food processing industries, chemical plants, paper mills and ceramic industries.
Other Advantages of Bio Coal Pellets Bio-coal pellets offer a powerful combination of logistical flexibility, environmental remediation, and economic resilience. Operationally, they are easy to store in bulk, use on demand, and provide a stable, cost-effective alternative to volatile fossil fuels like furnace oil and coal. Environmentally, they provide a carbon-neutral green fuel that slashes greenhouse gases and industrial particulate emissions while directly mitigating the crisis of open-field stubble burning. Strategically, this localized energy model leverages aggressive Government of India renewable mandates to substitute expensive imported coal, strengthening national energy security and keeping capital within the domestic rural economy.
Shelf Life of Bio Coal Pellets Unlike thermal coal, non-torrefied bio-coal pellets are highly hygroscopic and readily absorb atmospheric moisture, resulting in a relatively short shelf life of approximately three months under standard atmospheric conditions. To preserve product integrity and prevent the pellets from swelling or crumbling, they must be stored in a fully enclosed, dry, and well-ventilated space. Mechanically, we need to mitigate this storage risk by operating on a rapid 'just-in-time' supply cycle—guaranteeing shipment to the client within one week of production. For transit, the pellets are transported via enclosed vehicles or trucks secured with heavy-duty, waterproof tarpaulins to completely isolate the cargo from external moisture and rain. This will work well with our goal of supplying Bio Coal Pellets to near by Power Plants that take delivery on a daily basis.
Low Polluting Non Torrefied Bio Coal Pellet manufacturing is a Low Polluting Industry falling in the Green Category

Critical Aspect This is a critical aspect for our business. Most Pellet Manufacturer failures are due to weakness in this area.
Seasonality Agricultural raw material is seasonal. Every season during harvest, we will have a short duration (a month) of time during which the raw material will have to be collected, baled, transported, and stored in huge quantities for use in the next several months. This is when the raw material will be available at an attractive price too. We get two seasons each year - Paddy season and Wheat season.
Storage Requirements Bales of our raw material (mainly paddy & wheat straws) can take about 1.2 bigha per 1000 Metric Tons of space to stack in square bales. Our storage requirement would be about 3000 to 4000 metric tons each season to be used till the next season. That comes to about 5 bighas of Raw Material storage space.

  • Third Party Providers

    • Easy and Scalable.
    • Can be costly.

    • Where we will have both Third Party Providers as well as one or more baler to do our own Direct Field Collection. This is our preferred approach.

11. Manufacturing Process

The manufacturing process of agri residue biomass pellets transforms loose biomass into high density solid fuel through mechanical processing. The outcome is uniform size, low moisture, high calorific vlue, and efficient compustion property pellets.

Stage Process Description
Raw Material Procurement Agricultural residues such as Paddy straw, Wheat straw, Paddy husk, and Mustard stalk are collected from farms using Tractor, Rake, Baler, and Labor and stored in covered sheds.
Pre-Cleaning (If Required) Removal of stones, metal pieces, and impurities using magnetic separators and screens.
Shredding Raw material should have a moisture level of 12%. Its fed into Shredder machines that reduces the size of the straw after shredding to between 2 to 3 centimeters
Grinding Shredded material is fed into Hammer Mill to grind and further reduce the size of the material to about 3 millimeter.
Pelletizing Ground material is fed into a Pellet Mill. Under high pressure and temperature, rollers compress the material through a die to form cylindrical pellets. The lignin content in the biomass works as a binder at those temperatures.
Cooling Freshly made pellets exit at a high temperature (70 to 90 degrees celcius). A Counter Flow Cooler reduces temperature to near ambient levels.
Screenig Vibrating screen separates fines and broken pellets, which are recycled back to the pellet mill.
Loading Finished pellets are loose loaded into a truck with a loader. The truck should be a closed truck that can protect the pellets from rain and sunlight.
Delivery Trucks deliver the pellets to clients site where three samples are taken for lab tests and pellets are unloaded using the hydrolic tipper system in the truck.

Some other machines usedin between are

  • Belt onveyor/Screw Conveyor for Material Transfer
  • Dust Collector System for Pollution Control
  • PLC Based Control Panels for Automation and monitoring

21. Becoming Competent and the Lowest Cost Producer

We have three of our Biggest costs where we need to work to save besides Labor and Transportation.

1. Raw materical cost

  • Capitalize on Your Owned Logistics Fleet: Own biomass collection. And store it under cover. You have wisely allocated ₹65 Lakhs for a tractor, baler, rake, and loader. Do not let this sit idle outside of harvest windows. Use this fleet to establish a "Direct-from-Field" aggregation network.
  • Close the Loop on Dust Loss (Energy & Cost Reduction). Ensure your Proveg system integrates an efficient pulse-jet bag filter system to trap 100% of these fines. Instead of disposing of it, route this fine dust back into the mixer chamber. Because fine particles have a massive surface-area-to-volume ratio, reintroducing them acts as a natural binder. This drastically lowers friction inside the Yulong XGJ560 pellet machines, extending the lifespan of your ring dies and reducing your heavy ₹0.60/kg spares cost.

2. Electricity Expense

  • Transition to Solar Open Access (Decarbonizing Power). Your variable grid tariff is ₹6.40/unit, but your fixed KVA demand charges add a whopping fixed burden annually. Bihar's renewable energy policies support Open Access for industrial consumers with a connected load above 100 kW. You can enter into a long-term Power Purchase Agreement (PPA) with a third-party solar developer to wheel solar energy directly to your plant. Solar open access drops your variable per-unit cost down to roughly **₹4.00–₹4.50/unit**, dropping your annual power bill while allowing you to market your bio-coal as a 100% green-processed fuel.
  • Install rooftop solar to offset daytime load.

3. Spares, Maintenance and general

  • Use VFDs on hammer mill, conveyors and feeders.
  • Maintain die life aggressively.
  • Upgrade to a 3-Shift System in Year 2. Once your supply chain stabilizes, your current 18-hour layout leaves the plant idle for 6 hours a day while still accumulating fixed land lease and KVA demand charges. Moving to a continuous 24-hour cycle (3 shifts) will allow you to process 12,000+ tons of biomass annually, diluting your fixed structural costs to the absolute minimum possible and securing your status as the lowest-cost producer in the market.
